ORIGINAL ARTICLE

Clustering data with partial background information
Chien‑Liang Liu1 · Wen‑Hoar Hsaio2 · Tao‑Hsing Chang3 · Hsuan‑Hsun Li4
Received: 25 December 2016 / Accepted: 20 January 2018 / Published online: 5 February 2018 © Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2018
Abstract Clustering with partial supervision background information or semi-supervised clustering, learning from a combination of both labeled and unlabeled data, has received a lot of attention over the last decade. The supervisory information is usually used as the constraints to bias clustering towards a good region of search space. This paper proposes a semi-supervised algorithm, called constrained non-negative matrix factorization (Constrained-NMF), with a few labeled examples as constraints to improve performance. The proposed algorithm is a matrix factorization algorithm, in which initialization of matrices is required at the beginning. Although the benefits of good initialization are well-known, randomized seeding of basis matrix and coefficient matrix is still the standard approach for many non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) algorithms. This work devises an algorithm called entropy-based weighted semi-supervised fuzzy c-means (EWSS-FCM) algorithm to initialize the seeds. The experimental results indicate that the proposed Constrained-NMF can benefit from the initialization obtained from EWSS-FCM, which emphasizes the role of labeled examples and automatically weights them during the course of clustering. This work considers labeled examples in the objective functions to devise the two algorithms, in which the labeled information is propagated to unlabeled examples iteratively. We further analyze the proposed Constrained-NMF and give convergence justifications. The experiments are conducted on five real data sets, and experimental results indicate that the proposed algorithm generally outperforms the other alternatives.
Keywords  Clustering · Fuzzy clustering · Semi-supervised learning · Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF)

1 Introduction
Clustering with partial supervision background information or semi-supervised clustering, learning from a combination of both labeled and unlabeled data, has received a lot of attention over the last decade [18, 54, 68]. One of the reasons is that semi-supervised clustering can use a few labeled examples along with a large amount of unlabeled data to learn a robust model. Moreover, semi-supervised clustering provides a means for the experimenters to incorporate prior knowledge into the objective function, so that the original problem can be encoded as a constrained optimization problem. Apparently, supervised learning is one of the most commonly used and successful types of machine learning, but supervised learning algorithms require sufficient labeled examples to infer a classification function. Data labeling is a time-consuming process and it is often expensive to acquire sufficient labeled examples in real world applications. In contrast, unlabeled data is relatively easy to collect, and many algorithms as well as experimental results have

demonstrated that unlabeled data can significantly improve learning accuracy in certain practical problems [13, 45].
Semi-supervised learning methods can be further classified into semi-supervised classification and semi-supervised clustering. Semi-supervised classification employs labeled data along with unlabeled data to construct a more accurate classifier; while semi-supervised clustering uses a small amount of labeled data to bias the clustering of unlabeled data. Various semi-supervised algorithms have been proposed, including co-training [13, 53], semi-supervised Naive Bayes [31, 45], transductive support vector machines (TSVM) [32], graph-based approaches [12, 25], and clustering-based approaches [4, 5, 51, 52, 66]. More detailed literature survey about semi-supervised learning algorithms can refer to the survey conducted by Zhu [67].
Semi-supervised learning must use enormous unlabeled examples to improve performance, and unlabeled examples have to share the same distribution as labeled ones, otherwise using unlabeled examples may harm classification performance. Cozman and Cohen [20] pointed out that unlabeled data can degrade the performance of a classifier when there are discrepancies between modeling assumptions used to build the classifier and the actual model that generates the data. Consequently, semi-supervised learning algorithms generally follow smoothness and cluster assumptions to model the structure of the underlying distribution of data [65].
Clustering is one of the most commonly used data mining techniques. The goal of clustering is to automatically assign objects into groups so that objects from the same cluster are more similar to each other than objects from different clusters. Additionally, clustering is an unsupervised learning method, so it does not require labeled data to train a model. Jain [30] has summarized existing well-known clustering methods, and discussed the major challenges and key issues in designing clustering algorithms. Practically, it is inappropriate to crisply assign an object into a unique group or category. For example, a digital image of vehicle, landscape and aircraft belongs to several categories with some possibilities. In text processing, a news article may comprise multiple topics, such as politics, economy and sports. Since the fuzzy theory was proposed by Zadeh [62], it has shown that fuzzy sets and possibility theory provide a homogeneous framework for the representation of both imprecise or uncertain information with a flexible interpretation [14]. In the last decade, the methodologies for combining fuzzy theory and machine learning algorithms have attracted a lot of attention [28, 55, 57].
Lee and Seung have previously shown that non-negativity is a useful constraint for matrix factorization and devised a matrix factorization algorithm called non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) to learn a parts-based representation of the data [38, 39]. The problem can be formulated as factorizing a non-negative matrix into two non-negative matrices, basis matrix and coefficient matrix  . Recent

studies have exploited the connections between NMF and some clustering algorithms, providing theoretical foundation for using NMF to cluster data. For example, it has been shown that NMF is equivalent to a relaxed form of K-means clustering [23], in which matrix contains centroid information and matrix contains cluster membership indicators. Furthermore, the entries in the coefficient matrix are non-negative, explaining why clustering information revealed from can be further viewed as a soft assignment of clustering, where each data object can belong to two or more clusters with different membership degrees. Additionally, matrix factorization can be viewed as discovering latent information from the data collection, and it has been applied to many application domains. The applications include, but are not limited to, text clustering [9, 52], collaborative filtering [37, 50, 61], and bioinformatics [22, 35].
The NMF is an unsupervised learning, and Lee and Seung [39] proposed two cost functions for NMF, which are based on Euclidean distance and Kullback–Leibler divergence, respectively. This work uses Kullback–Leibler divergence as the cost function, and considers labeled examples in the cost function to devise an algorithm called Constrained-NMF. Although the benefits of good initialization are well-known, randomized seeding of and is still the standard approach for many NMF algorithms. Besides determining initial configuration randomly, many research studies apply K-means algorithm to initialize the seeds owing to the simplicity and efficiency of K-means. For example, Wang et al. [52] used K-means to initialize their proposed algorithm called penalized matrix factorization (PMF). This work considers the role of labeled examples in the semi-supervised learning algorithm and the flexibility of soft assignment in the clustering algorithm to devise an algorithm called entropy-based weighted semi-supervised fuzzy c-means (EWSS-FCM) to initialize the proposed Constrained-NMF. The experimental results indicate that the proposed Constrained-NMF can benefit from the initialization obtained from EWSS-FCM, which emphasizes the role of labeled examples and automatically weights them during the course of clustering.
This work focuses on semi-supervised clustering, and the main contributions of this paper are listed as follows. First, this work devises a semi-supervised learning algorithm. In algorithm design, we consider partial background information as constraint to formulate the proposed objective function as a constrained optimization problem. Besides, this work analyzes the proposed Constrained-NMF, and gives convergence justifications. Second, we propose an algorithm called EWSS-FCM to initialize Constrained-NMF. The experimental results indicate that using EWSS-FCM as initialization method can improve performance of ConstrainedNMF. Finally, we use several semi-supervised learning algorithms to compare with the proposed Constrained-NMF on

2 Related work
Semi-supervised learning often uses one of the following assumptions to model the structure of the underlying distribution of data [65]. First, nearby points are likely to have the same label, namely, the classification function should be smooth with respect to the intrinsic structure collectively revealed by known labeled and unlabeled data. Many studies view the smoothness as an constrained optimization problem and regularize the problem at hand [21, 33, 34]. Second, points on the same structure are likely to have the same label. As a result, the classification functions are naturally defined only on the sub-manifold in question rather than the total ambient space [7, 19, 69]. This argument is often called the cluster assumption.
Many semi-supervised learning methods are extended from existing learning algorithm by using optimization with constraints technique to view labeled examples as constraints. Then, considering new constraints in the objective function of existing algorithm becomes a new optimization problem. For instance, many clustering algorithms aim at the minimization of the cost function, which involves distortion measure between the objects and the cluster representatives. Thus, semi-supervised clustering can formulate labeled examples as regularization terms of optimization problems. This technique has been widely used by many researchers [15, 31, 44, 52, 56]. For instance, Bouchachia and Pedrycz [15] developed a semi-supervised clustering algorithm, which extends the objective function of Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) [11] to encode labeled data as an additional regularization term. Miyamoto et al. [44] used the same technique in fuzzy semi-supervised clustering. Wang et al. [52] considered must-link and cannot-link as prior knowledge and used a penalty term to encode prior knowledge to develop a penalized matrix factorization (PMF) algorithm, which is a semi-supervised learning method extended from matrix factorization. Wang and Davidson [56] proposed a flexible and generalized framework for constrained spectral clustering, which can be formulated as a constrained optimization problem by adding a constraint to the original objective function of spectral clustering. Ji et al. [31] proposed a semi-supervised clustering model that incorporates

prior knowledge about documents’ membership for document cluster analysis. The prior knowledge is provided in the form of indicating several pairs of documents which the user wishes to be grouped into the same cluster. They employed normalized cut [47] as cost function and introduced a penalty term with a penalty coefficient matrix to incorporate the user’s prior knowledge on the data set. Then the document clustering task is accomplished by finding the cluster set that globally optimizes the constrained cost function.
Moreover, graph-based approach is another popular method in semi-supervised learning. The labeled and unlabeled examples are defined on a graph where nodes represent the labeled and unlabeled examples, and edges reflect the similarities of pairs of nodes. The general idea is to design cost functions on labeled examples, such that the predictions on the labeled nodes are close to the given labels. Meanwhile, graph-based methods assume label smoothness over the graph, which can be encoded as an additional regularization term. For instance, Blum and Chawla [12] viewed the semi-supervised learning as a graph minimum cut problem, in which infinite weights are given to labeled examples to ensure the predictions on labeled ones are correct. Zhou et al. [65] considered local and global consistency by using the normalized Laplacian. Many graph-based methods are mentioned and discussed in the literature survey conducted by Zhu [67].
Although unsupervised learning approaches do not need labeled data in the course of clustering process, proper seeding biases clustering towards a good region of the search space [4]. Wagstaff et al. [51] devised a semi-supervised variant of K-means called COP-KMeans to use constraints in order to represent background knowledge. Two constraints are must-link (i.e. two instances must be together in the same cluster) and cannot-link (i.e. two instances must be in different clusters). These constraints are used during clustering to generate a partition that satisfies all given constraints. Basu et al. [4] introduced two semi-supervised variants of K-means clustering that use initial labeled data for seeding. These two algorithms are Seeded-KMeans and ConstrainedKMeans. In Seeded-KMeans, the seeds are only used to initialize the K-means algorithm, and they are not used in the clustering algorithm. In Constrained-KMeans, the seeds are used to initialize centers and keep the grouping of labeled data unchanged throughout the clustering process.
Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) [11] is a typical clustering algorithm, which benefits from fuzzy logic [63] to allow data to belong to two or more clusters with different degrees of membership. Each object has a fuzzy membership to indicate the degree of belonging to each cluster. The soft assignment mechanism is more flexible than hard assignment clustering algorithm such as K-means, since hard assignment may lead to some problems for those objects that are located in the boundaries among cluster centers. The FCM is one of the most popular methods used in pattern recognition.

As the success and popularity of the FCM algorithm, many fuzzy clustering [3, 17, 27] and semi-supervised fuzzy clustering methods [2, 6, 8, 26, 41, 43, 46, 49, 60] have been devised and applied to image or document clustering problems. Klose and Kruse [36] have reviewed many semi-supervised learning approaches extended from FCM algorithm, and proposed an evolutionary algorithm that incorporates labeled and unlabeled examples into its objective function to construct fuzzy rule base.
Besides the methods mentioned above, another interesting strategy is to emphasize the role of labeled data to devise semi-supervised learning algorithms. For instance, Labzour et al. [49] proposed a semi-supervised point-prototype clustering algorithm called ssPPC, which over-partitions the unlabeled data and aligns the resulting clusters with labeled data. Then, the unlabeled examples are labeled based on their memberships to the clusters. Bensaid et al. [8] devised an algorithm called semi-supervised FCM (ssFCM), which is an extension of FCM. The idea of ssFCM algorithm is to trust the labeled data by using weighting mechanism, in which the clusters of labeled data are fixed as given during the clustering process. Practically, ssFCM requires experts to determine the weights of labeled data [8]. As mentioned above, this work proposes an algorithm called EWSSFCM to initialize the proposed Constrained-NMF. Inspired by ssFCM, the proposed EWSS-FCM uses given labeled examples and applies the weighting mechanism to emphasize their roles. The main difference between the proposed EWSS-FCM and ssFCM is that the weights of labeled examples are automatically determined from the structure of data instead of determining by experts as used in ssFCM. The experimental results indicate that the proposed EWSS-FCM generally outperforms ssFCM.
3 EWSS‑FCM and constrained‑NMF
This section lists the notations that are used in the following sections, and introduces the proposed EWSS-FCM and Constrained-NMF algorithms.

3.1 Notation
The notations that will be used in the following sections are listed in Table 1. Each data object i is represented as a feature vector with length M. The data collection is represented by = [ 1, 2, … , N] , indicating that the number of data objects is N and i ∈ ℝM . This work focuses on semi-supervised learning, so a small amount of labeled examples is available in the data collection. We use l to denote labeled data and use u to represent unlabeled data. Thus, the whole data set can be divided into two partitions, namely, = l ∪ u . The goal is to partition the data collection into K clusters, each of which has a mean vector k(1 ≤ k ≤ K) ∈ ℝM . Similarly, we use a matrix to denote all of the mean vectors, namely, = [ 1, 2, … , K] . Additionally, each data object has a membership vector to indicate its membership degree belonging to the K clusters. A matrix = [ 1, 2, … , N] is used to represent membership degrees of all data, in which i ∈ [0, 1]K.

The FCM is an unsupervised learning method, so it does not require labeled data during the course of clustering. Practically, a small amount of labeled examples may be available, inspiring us to use the available labeled examples to improve performance. This work uses available labeled examples for two purposes. First, these labeled examples can determine the initial guess of the centers. Practically, clustering algorithms such as K-means and FCM are sensitive to the initial guess. Thus, the seeds can provide a better guess for the algorithm to cluster the data. Second, these labeled examples should bias the clustering towards a better searching space during the course of clustering.

The proposed EWSS-FCM comprises the following steps. First, the EWSS-FCM applies FCM to cluster all data objects, and uses the clustering results to calculate the entropy of each data object. Entropy is commonly used in measuring uncertainty and represents the amount of information as measured in bits [58]. Note that the range of entropy is from 0 to log2 K , where K is the number of possible values for a discrete random variable and can be considered as the number of clusters in this paper. This work proposes to use entropy to represent the confidence of each data object belonging to its corresponding prototype. For each data object, the lower the entropy, the higher the confidence is recognized. Furthermore, we emphasize those data objects having high entropy and dilute ones having low entropy during the course of clustering. The NMF is an unsupervised learning method, but the proposed constrained-NMF algorithm simultaneously considers unlabeled and labeled examples in the cost function as shown in Eq. (9). Next, the optimization can become Eq. (10) in which labeled examples and unlabeled examples are considered separately. The key idea is to view the clusters of labeled examples as the prior knowledge of the learning algorithm and they are the optimal soft assignments of clustering for labeled examples in Eq. (10).
It is apparent that two block variables are involved in the cost function and solving Eq. (10) is intrinsically a non-convex problem, but is convex in only one block variable while fixing the other block variable. As a result, this work uses block coordinate descent to optimize the objective function listed in Eq. (10). The key idea behind the block coordinate descent method is to handle problems with multiple blocks of variables, and update one of them once. Moreover, convergence of block coordinate descent has been proved while the cost function is continuously differentiable and the minimum of this cost function is uniquely attained [10]. Consequently, applying block coordinate descent to Eq. (10), the minimum of this cost function is uniquely attained when fixing one block variable such that the convergence of the proposed Constrained-NMF algorithm can be achieved. Xu and Yin [59] also demonstrated that block coordinate descent method can lead to superior performance in both speed and solution quality.

4.2 Evaluation measurements
We compare the generated clusters by using the F1 cluster evaluation measure [42] and Jaccard Index [29]. The F1 cluster evaluation measure considers both precision and recall, where precision and recall here are computed over pairs of data instances of which the two label assignments either agree or disagree. The Jaccard Index, also known as the Jaccard similarity coefficient, is a measurement used for comparing

• True Positives (TP) 	  The clustering algorithm placed the two data instances
in a pair into the same cluster, and data corpus has them in the same class. • False Positives (FP) 	  The clustering algorithm placed the two data instances in a pair into the same cluster, but data corpus has them in differing classes. • True Negatives (TN) 	  The clustering algorithm placed the two data instances in a pair into differing clusters, and data corpus has them in differing classes. • False Negatives (FN) 	  The clustering algorithm placed the two data instances in a pair into differing clusters, but data corpus has them in the same class.

This work randomly selected examples as the labeled ones and the rest of examples are unlabeled examples. 4.5 Discussion
This work conducts experiments on five data sets, and the experimental results indicate that the proposed method generally outperforms the other methods. Even though the class boundaries are not clear, the proposed method performs well. Besides text data sets, the experimental results indicate that the proposed method also performs well on image data set. Consequently, the proposed method can use available labeled examples to improve performance. We further analyze and discuss the experiments in this section.
Furthermore, we conduct experiments to compare the proposed EWSS-FCM with K-means and FCM. Figure 4 summaries the experimental results, where EWSS-FCM uses only 1% of target examples as labeled ones. We use the average of ten experiments as the performance result. The experimental results indicate that EWSS-FCM can significantly outperform K-means and FCM, even though only a few labeled examples are available. As a result, EWSS-FCM with a few labeled examples can improve performance as compared with K-means and FCM. The semi-supervised learning experiments also indicate that EWSS-FCM can function properly on some data sets.

Among the comparison methods, PMF is a semi-supervised learning method extended from matrix factorization. Several differences exist between PMF and Constrained-NMF. First, PMF is derived from K-means, so it uses the squared Euclidean distance as the cost function; while the proposed method uses the Kullback–Leibler divergence. Second, PMF uses K-means to initialize the parameters; while the proposed method uses EWSS-FCM. Third, PMF uses a penalty term to encode prior knowledge; while the proposed method uses the prior knowledge during the course of clustering. The experimental results indicate that the proposed method outperforms PMF. One of the reasons is that using Kullback–Leibler divergence as the cost function is more appropriate than Euclidean distance in document analysis problem, in which a document collection generally contains thousands of terms. Euclidean distance is inappropriate for high-dimensional data sets, since not all the measured variables are important for understanding the underlying phenomena of interest [24]. Additionally, the experimental results also indicate that following clustering assumption with labeled examples in the objective function is an effective way to propagate labeled information to unlabeled examples.
In summary, the main reasons why the proposed Constrained-NMF outperforms other alternatives in the experiments are listed as follows:

• The benefits of good initialization are obvious and well known. This work devises EWSS-FCM algorithm to initialize the seeds of Constrained-NMF, giving a good start point to function properly during the clustering process.
• This work considers labeled examples in the cost function, and reformulates the cost function by separately considering the labeled ones and unlabeled ones. Apparently, the clusters of labeled examples can be viewed as the prior knowledge of the learning algorithm and considered as the optimal soft assignments of clustering for labeled examples in the course of optimization. Through the propagation of the optimal soft assignments of clustering to unlabeled examples iteratively, we can obtain more reasonable clustering results.
• The proposed method uses Kullback–Leibler divergence as the cost function, which is more appropriate than Euclidean distance in document analysis problem. As a result, the proposed method functions properly in the experiments.
5 Conclusion
This work devises a semi-supervised learning algorithm called Constrained-NMF with a few labeled examples and a large amount of unlabeled data for data clustering. The initialization of Constrained-NMF is achieved by using the proposed EWSS-FCM. Using EWSS-FCM to perform initialization can provide a good guess for the initial configuration of the proposed Constrained-NMF. In algorithm design, both algorithms consider available labeled examples in the course of clustering, and use the update rules to propagate labeled information to unlabeled examples iteratively.
